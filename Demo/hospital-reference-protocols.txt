MEDBRIDGE AI — SAMPLE HOSPITAL REFERENCE PROTOCOLS
====================================================
DEMO / PROTOTYPE CONTENT ONLY.
This file is sample data for testing the RAG knowledge base in a student
capstone project. It is NOT a validated clinical protocol. In any real
deployment, replace this file with your hospital's own approved first-aid
and treatment guidelines, reviewed by a licensed physician. Medicine
choices and dosages here are general public first-aid knowledge only and
must never be used for actual patient treatment.
====================================================

CONDITION: Fracture (suspected bone break)
SYMPTOMS: Severe localized pain, swelling, deformity or abnormal angle of
the limb, inability to bear weight or move the area, bruising, possible
visible bone in open fractures.
VITALS TO MONITOR: Pulse and skin color/temperature below the injury site
(check for circulation loss), pain level, presence of numbness or
tingling, blood pressure if blood loss is visible.
TEMPORARY (FIRST-AID) TREATMENT: Do not attempt to straighten or realign
the limb. Immobilize the area using a splint or rigid support and
padding, keep the limb still, apply a cold pack wrapped in cloth to
reduce swelling (avoid direct ice contact), elevate the limb if possible,
control any external bleeding with clean pressure. Do not give food or
water if surgery may be needed later.
MEDICINES (GENERAL FIRST AID ONLY): Basic oral pain relief such as
paracetamol may be used for pain if locally approved and no allergy or
contraindication exists; avoid NSAIDs if a bleeding injury is suspected.
PERMANENT / DEFINITIVE TREATMENT: X-ray confirmation, followed by closed
reduction and casting for simple fractures, or surgical fixation (plates,
screws, rods) for displaced or complex fractures, done by an orthopedic
specialist.
RECOMMENDATION: Refer to urban/specialist care for imaging and definitive
management. Do not weight-bear on the limb until cleared.

CONDITION: Fever
SYMPTOMS: Body temperature above normal (typically over 38°C / 100.4°F),
chills, sweating, headache, muscle aches, weakness, sometimes confusion
in very high fevers.
VITALS TO MONITOR: Body temperature (recheck every 2-4 hours), pulse
rate, hydration status, respiratory rate, level of consciousness in
young children or elderly patients.
TEMPORARY (FIRST-AID) TREATMENT: Rest, plenty of fluids, light clothing,
tepid sponging if temperature is very high, monitor for signs of
dehydration or worsening symptoms.
MEDICINES (GENERAL FIRST AID ONLY): Paracetamol (acetaminophen) is
commonly used as a general fever reducer where locally approved;
ibuprofen may be used as an alternative in adults without contraindication.
Follow local dosing guidelines based on age and weight.
PERMANENT / DEFINITIVE TREATMENT: Depends on underlying cause — antibiotics
if a bacterial infection is confirmed, antimalarials if malaria is
confirmed by test, supportive care and monitoring for viral causes.
RECOMMENDATION: If fever persists beyond 3 days, exceeds 40°C, or is
accompanied by rash, stiff neck, difficulty breathing, or confusion,
refer immediately for urgent evaluation.

CONDITION: Common cold
SYMPTOMS: Runny or blocked nose, sneezing, sore throat, mild cough, mild
fatigue, low-grade or no fever.
VITALS TO MONITOR: Temperature, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation if
available and breathing seems labored.
TEMPORARY (FIRST-AID) TREATMENT: Rest, warm fluids, steam inhalation,
saline nasal rinse, gargling warm salt water for sore throat.
MEDICINES (GENERAL FIRST AID ONLY): Over-the-counter decongestants or
antihistamines may relieve symptoms where locally approved; paracetamol
for associated discomfort or mild fever.
PERMANENT / DEFINITIVE TREATMENT: Usually self-limiting and resolves in
7-10 days without specific treatment; antibiotics are not effective
against viral colds and should not be used unless a bacterial
complication is diagnosed.
RECOMMENDATION: Refer only if symptoms worsen, persist beyond 10 days, or
if breathing difficulty, high fever, or ear/sinus pain develops.

CONDITION: Diarrhea and dehydration
SYMPTOMS: Frequent loose or watery stools, abdominal cramping, nausea,
possible vomiting, signs of dehydration (dry mouth, sunken eyes, reduced
urination, dizziness, lethargy).
VITALS TO MONITOR: Blood pressure (may drop with dehydration), pulse rate
(may rise), skin turgor, urine output, level of alertness — especially
critical to monitor closely in infants and elderly patients.
TEMPORARY (FIRST-AID) TREATMENT: Oral rehydration solution (ORS) given in
small frequent sips, continue breastfeeding for infants, avoid solid
heavy foods initially, monitor for worsening dehydration signs.
MEDICINES (GENERAL FIRST AID ONLY): Oral rehydration salts are the
primary intervention; zinc supplementation is commonly recommended for
children in many national protocols. Anti-diarrheal medicines are
generally avoided in suspected infectious diarrhea without medical
guidance.
PERMANENT / DEFINITIVE TREATMENT: IV fluids for moderate-to-severe
dehydration, antibiotics only if a specific bacterial cause is confirmed,
investigation for underlying cause if diarrhea is prolonged or recurrent.
RECOMMENDATION: Refer urgently if there is blood in stool, high fever,
signs of severe dehydration, or if the patient is an infant, elderly, or
immunocompromised.

CONDITION: Hypertensive emergency (severely high blood pressure)
SYMPTOMS: Severe headache, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of
breath, confusion, nosebleed, in severe cases seizures or loss of
consciousness.
VITALS TO MONITOR: Blood pressure (recheck frequently), pulse rate,
respiratory rate, level of consciousness, pupil response if altered
consciousness is present.
TEMPORARY (FIRST-AID) TREATMENT: Keep the patient calm and seated or
semi-reclined, loosen tight clothing, avoid sudden position changes,
do not give any medication without protocol guidance, prepare for urgent
transfer.
MEDICINES (GENERAL FIRST AID ONLY): No self-administered medication
should be given at first-aid level for a hypertensive emergency without
direct medical instruction, due to risk of dangerous blood pressure
swings.
PERMANENT / DEFINITIVE TREATMENT: Controlled IV or oral antihypertensive
therapy titrated by a physician, investigation for underlying cause
(kidney disease, pregnancy-related hypertension, medication
non-adherence), long-term blood pressure management plan.
RECOMMENDATION: Treat as a medical emergency — refer for immediate
specialist care; this is not a condition to manage at first-aid level
alone.

CONDITION: Asthma attack
SYMPTOMS: Wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, coughing,
difficulty speaking in full sentences, visible distress or use of
accessory breathing muscles.
VITALS TO MONITOR: Respiratory rate, oxygen saturation if available,
pulse rate, ability to speak, level of consciousness in severe attacks.
TEMPORARY (FIRST-AID) TREATMENT: Help the patient sit upright, stay calm,
loosen tight clothing, assist with their prescribed reliever inhaler if
they have one, avoid exertion, monitor closely for worsening.
MEDICINES (GENERAL FIRST AID ONLY): A fast-acting (reliever) inhaler such
as salbutamol, if already prescribed to the patient, may be used per
their existing prescription; do not give a new inhaler prescription at
first-aid level.
PERMANENT / DEFINITIVE TREATMENT: Long-term inhaled corticosteroids and
bronchodilator therapy managed by a physician, identification and
avoidance of triggers, an individualized asthma action plan.
RECOMMENDATION: Refer urgently if there is no improvement after inhaler
use, lips or fingertips turn blue, or the patient cannot speak in full
sentences.

CONDITION: Minor wound or cut
SYMPTOMS: Broken skin, bleeding, pain at the site, possible surrounding
redness or swelling if infected.
VITALS TO MONITOR: Amount and rate of bleeding, signs of infection
(increasing redness, warmth, pus, fever) over following days.
TEMPORARY (FIRST-AID) TREATMENT: Clean the wound with clean water, apply
gentle direct pressure with a clean cloth to stop bleeding, apply a
sterile dressing, keep the wound clean and dry.
MEDICINES (GENERAL FIRST AID ONLY): A topical antiseptic may be applied
per local protocol; oral pain relief such as paracetamol if needed.
PERMANENT / DEFINITIVE TREATMENT: Sutures or wound closure for deep cuts,
tetanus vaccination review, antibiotics only if infection develops.
RECOMMENDATION: Refer if the wound is deep, won't stop bleeding, shows
signs of infection, or if the patient's tetanus vaccination is not
up to date.

CONDITION: Burns (minor to moderate)
SYMPTOMS: Redness, pain, blistering, swelling at the burn site; in more
severe burns, white or charred skin and reduced pain sensation.
VITALS TO MONITOR: Extent and depth of the burn (percentage of body
surface area), pain level, signs of shock (rapid pulse, low blood
pressure) in larger burns.
TEMPORARY (FIRST-AID) TREATMENT: Cool the burn under clean running water
for 10-20 minutes (do not use ice), remove tight clothing/jewelry near
the area before swelling starts, cover loosely with a clean non-stick
dressing, do not apply butter, oil, or toothpaste.
MEDICINES (GENERAL FIRST AID ONLY): Oral pain relief such as paracetamol
for discomfort; do not apply any ointment not approved by local protocol
until assessed.
PERMANENT / DEFINITIVE TREATMENT: Specialist wound care or skin grafting
for severe/deep burns, IV fluid management for large burns, infection
monitoring and dressing changes over time.
RECOMMENDATION: Refer immediately for burns larger than a hand's size,
burns on the face/hands/genitals, or any burn with charred or white skin.

CONDITION: Chest pain (possible cardiac event)
SYMPTOMS: Chest pressure, tightness, or pain (may radiate to arm, jaw, or
back), shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, lightheadedness.
VITALS TO MONITOR: Pulse rate and rhythm, blood pressure, respiratory
rate, oxygen saturation if available, level of consciousness.
TEMPORARY (FIRST-AID) TREATMENT: Keep the patient calm and seated or
semi-reclined, loosen tight clothing, do not let them exert themselves,
prepare for urgent transfer — this is a time-critical emergency.
MEDICINES (GENERAL FIRST AID ONLY): Chewable aspirin may be considered
per local emergency protocol only if there is no known allergy or
bleeding risk and local guidance permits it; this should not be given
without protocol authorization.
PERMANENT / DEFINITIVE TREATMENT: ECG and cardiac enzyme testing,
clot-dissolving therapy or emergency angioplasty for confirmed heart
attack, ongoing cardiac medication and monitoring.
RECOMMENDATION: Treat as a medical emergency and arrange the fastest
possible transfer to specialist cardiac care; do not delay for further
first-aid steps.

CONDITION: Suspected snake bite
SYMPTOMS: Puncture marks, localized pain and swelling, possible nausea,
difficulty breathing, drooping eyelids, difficulty swallowing, bleeding
from gums or wounds in some envenomations.
VITALS TO MONITOR: Respiratory rate and effort, blood pressure, pulse,
swelling progression at the bite site, level of consciousness.
TEMPORARY (FIRST-AID) TREATMENT: Keep the patient still and calm, keep
the bitten limb immobilized and below heart level, remove tight
clothing/jewelry near the site, do not cut, suck, or apply ice to the
wound, do not apply a tight tourniquet — use a firm but not restrictive
pressure bandage if trained to do so per local protocol.
MEDICINES (GENERAL FIRST AID ONLY): No first-aid medication should be
self-administered; antivenom must only be given by trained medical staff
after assessment.
PERMANENT / DEFINITIVE TREATMENT: Species-appropriate antivenom
administration, supportive care for breathing or bleeding complications,
monitoring in a medical facility for at least 24 hours.
RECOMMENDATION: Treat as a medical emergency in all cases — transfer
immediately regardless of symptom severity, as effects can worsen
rapidly.

CONDITION: Diabetic emergency (suspected hypoglycemia)
SYMPTOMS: Sudden sweating, shakiness, confusion, irritability, rapid
heartbeat, hunger, in severe cases loss of consciousness or seizures.
VITALS TO MONITOR: Blood glucose level if a glucometer is available,
pulse rate, level of consciousness, repeat glucose check after
treatment.
TEMPORARY (FIRST-AID) TREATMENT: If the patient is conscious and able to
swallow, give a fast-acting sugar source (juice, sugar water, glucose
tablets); recheck alertness after 10-15 minutes; if unconscious, do not
give anything by mouth and seek emergency care immediately.
MEDICINES (GENERAL FIRST AID ONLY): Oral glucose/sugar for conscious
patients; glucagon injection or IV glucose is a medical-staff-only
intervention for unconscious patients.
PERMANENT / DEFINITIVE TREATMENT: Review and adjustment of the patient's
diabetes medication regimen, diabetes education, ongoing glucose
monitoring plan.
RECOMMENDATION: Refer urgently if the patient does not regain full
alertness after sugar intake, or immediately if unconscious.
